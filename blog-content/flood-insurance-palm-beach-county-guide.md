Most homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage. FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program offers flood policies for homeowners, renters, and businesses. Building and contents coverage have distinct terms and deductibles. [FEMA flood policy guide](https://www.floodsmart.gov/get-insured/buy-a-policy).

## Read the proposed coverage

Bring a description of the property and the belongings you want to discuss. Ask a licensed professional to identify the applicable form and explain the proposed policy in writing.

- Does the proposal address the building, belongings, or both?
- What limits and deductibles apply to each part?
- Which exclusions matter for the way you use the property?
- When would coverage start, and what conditions must be met first?
- Who handles policy changes, renewals, and claims?

Avoid treating every kind of water damage as the same insured event. Ask the insurer how the cause of a loss affects the coverage being discussed.

## Compare documents, not labels

If comparing an NFIP proposal with a private flood proposal, request the actual coverage documents for each. A name or premium alone does not tell you whether the proposals meet the same needs. This guide does not promise that either option is available, cheaper, or more suitable for a particular address.

## Prepare a useful record

Keep the quote date, quoted terms, source documents, and the professional's written answers together. Recheck the details before purchasing rather than relying on an earlier estimate.
