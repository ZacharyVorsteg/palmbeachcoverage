Prepare the household plan alongside the insurance paperwork. Florida's Division of Emergency Management recommends an emergency plan and stocked supplies, and directs residents to their county emergency-management program for current local information. [Florida Plan & Prepare resources](https://www.floridadisaster.org/planprepare/).

## Organize the documents

Save copies of declarations pages, policy endorsements, insurer contacts, and a record of important belongings where you can reach them if you leave home. Record questions that need an insurer's response, rather than assuming a document means a specific loss is covered.

## Ask the policy questions early

- Which policy covers the risks you are discussing?
- What exclusions and deductibles apply?
- When would a requested change actually become effective?
- How should a loss be reported, and what information should be retained?
- Who should be contacted if online access is unavailable?

Do not assume that coverage can be bought or changed immediately as a storm approaches. Confirm timing with the insurer; this guide does not state a universal binding restriction or deadline.

## Make the practical plan

Use the official preparedness links to review supplies, evacuation planning, and support for household members who may need assistance. Follow current instructions from local emergency officials rather than an old blog date.

Write down who will contact whom, how household members will reconnect, and where essential records will be stored. Review the plan with the people who will use it. Include pets and any accessibility needs in the discussion.

## After the immediate danger

Follow official safety instructions. Use the claims contact listed in the policy and ask the insurer what records it needs. This publication does not replace emergency instructions, inspect damage, or handle claims.
