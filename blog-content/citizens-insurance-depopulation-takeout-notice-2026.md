# Citizens Depopulation 2026: What to Do With Your Florida Takeout Notice

If you receive a Citizens depopulation notice, a private insurer has bid to take over your policy. You have 30 days to accept or reject the offer. Rejecting keeps you on Citizens — for now. Accepting moves you to the private carrier at a rate the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation has reviewed. Neither choice is automatically wrong; it depends on the premium difference and the carrier's financial strength.

---

## What Is the Citizens Depopulation Program?

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation was created by the Florida Legislature as the insurer of last resort — the option for homeowners who cannot find affordable coverage in the private market. For years, Citizens served that role quietly for a modest number of policyholders. Then came a string of active hurricane seasons, a litigation crisis, and a wave of private insurer insolvencies. By late 2023, Citizens was carrying roughly 1.4 million policies, according to Citizens Property Insurance Corporation's own reporting — a number the state considered financially dangerous for Florida taxpayers, who would ultimately absorb a Citizens shortfall through assessments.

The depopulation program is the mechanism to move those policies back into the private market. Florida law requires Citizens to run this program whenever private carriers are willing to take policies at rates the OIR considers reasonable. According to Citizens Property Insurance Corporation's official depopulation page, the program matches Citizens policyholders — both personal and commercial — with private insurers through a clearinghouse process that runs multiple times per year.

In May 2026, Citizens completed one of its largest single depopulation rounds: 11 private carriers submitted winning bids to absorb 184,212 policies, according to a Citizens board report from April 2026. The opt-out window for that round ran from April 25 through May 23. Homeowners who did nothing were moved automatically. Florida homeowners insurance depopulation rounds continued through the summer of 2026, and according to news coverage from mid-2026, Citizens policies have reached their lowest level in years as a result.

This is the environment your takeout notice arrives in. Understanding exactly what it means — and what your options are — matters more than it did two years ago.

## What a Takeout Notice Actually Says

When Citizens selects your policy for a depopulation round, you receive a written notice from Citizens (and often from the accepting carrier directly). The notice will tell you:

- **Which private carrier** has submitted a bid to take your policy
- **The proposed annual premium** from the private carrier
- **Your current Citizens premium** for comparison
- **The deadline** to opt out if you do not want the transfer — typically 30 days from the notice date
- **What happens if you do nothing** — the policy transfers automatically at the renewal date

One thing the notice will not always spell out clearly: the private carrier's rate may be higher than what you pay Citizens today. Under Florida OIR rules, a private insurer's takeout offer cannot exceed Citizens' rates by more than a defined ceiling — but that ceiling is not zero. Policyholders have reported takeout offers that are meaningfully more expensive than their Citizens premium. That gap is the central question you need to evaluate.

## The 30-Day Decision: Accept, Reject, or Shop

You have three real choices when a takeout notice arrives.

### Option 1: Accept the Transfer

If you do nothing, the transfer happens automatically. If you actively accept, the same result follows. The private carrier issues you a new policy, often with the same coverage terms as your Citizens policy but sometimes with different exclusions or endorsements. Read the full declarations page before assuming coverage is identical.

Accepting makes sense when:
- The new premium is within a few hundred dollars of your Citizens rate
- The accepting carrier has an A-rated or better financial strength rating from AM Best
- Your Citizens policy was already stripped-down coverage and the private policy offers broader protection

### Option 2: Reject the Transfer (Opt Out)

You can reject the takeout offer by notifying Citizens in writing within the opt-out window — typically 30 days. This keeps you on Citizens for the current policy term. There is no penalty for opting out once.

Rejecting makes sense when:
- The private carrier's rate is substantially higher and you cannot absorb the difference
- The accepting carrier has a weak financial strength rating
- You have already been shopping the private market and have a better quote in hand

One important caveat: continued eligibility for Citizens coverage is not automatic. According to Florida statute (Section 627.351) and Citizens' own eligibility guidelines, if a comparable private market option exists at or below 120% of your Citizens rate, Citizens may deem you ineligible for renewal. The depopulation program is partly designed to identify which policyholders have private market options available. Opting out once does not solve the problem indefinitely.

### Option 3: Shop the Entire Market Before the Deadline

This is the move most homeowners skip because the 30-day window feels short. It shouldn't be skipped. A licensed Florida agent can run your property through multiple admitted and surplus lines carriers in a few hours. You might find a private policy cheaper than both the takeout offer and your current Citizens rate — especially if you have a newer roof, recent wind mitigation features, or a property that private underwriters are now actively seeking.

For context on why the private market has been reopening for certain properties, see [Florida Homeowners Insurance Crisis Explained (2026)](https://palmbeachcoverage.com/blog/florida-homeowners-insurance-crisis-explained/) — the reforms that stabilized the market are the same ones making takeout offers possible at rates closer to Citizens'.

## What to Check About the Accepting Carrier

Not all private carriers bidding in Citizens depopulation rounds are equal. Before accepting any transfer, verify:

**Financial strength rating.** According to AM Best, insurers rated A- or better have demonstrated a strong ability to meet ongoing insurance obligations. A B+ or below warrants scrutiny. You can look up any carrier's AM Best rating at ambest.com — it is free and takes 60 seconds.

**Complaint ratio.** The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation publishes complaint data for every licensed insurer at floir.com. A carrier with an unusually high complaint ratio relative to its market share is a signal worth heeding before you hand them your policy.

**Reinsurance structure.** Florida carriers live and die by their reinsurance arrangements. A carrier that could not purchase sufficient reinsurance for the 2025-2026 hurricane season is a different risk than one with a full tower. Your licensed agent will know — ask directly.

**Policy form differences.** Citizens uses standardized policy forms. Some private carriers use forms with narrower coverage for items like roof damage, water intrusion, or mold. According to the Florida Department of Financial Services, consumers have the right to review the full policy form before accepting a transfer. Exercise it.

## Citizens Depopulation and the Citizens Clearinghouse

There are actually two separate Citizens mechanisms that can move your policy. The depopulation program (described above) is proactive — Citizens selects your policy and a private carrier bids on it. The Clearinghouse is a different system, required under Florida law, that runs at renewal time: before Citizens can renew your policy, it must check whether a private carrier will take you within certain rate parameters.

A separate law, SB 1028, expanded this Clearinghouse to commercial policies beginning in 2025. If you own commercial property currently insured by Citizens, you may be seeing Clearinghouse notices for the first time. For a full breakdown of SB 1028's commercial provisions, see [Florida SB 1028: Citizens Commercial Clearinghouse Explained](https://palmbeachcoverage.com/blog/citizens-clearinghouse-sb-1028-florida/).

The practical effect for homeowners: both the depopulation program and the Clearinghouse can trigger a policy move. Understanding which one you are dealing with changes what your deadline is and what your options are.

## What Happens If You Lose Citizens Eligibility

If Citizens determines that a private market option exists at or below the statutory threshold — and you decline it — Citizens can non-renew your policy at the end of the current term. This is not punitive; it is built into Florida statute. Citizens exists to serve homeowners who genuinely cannot find private market coverage. When private market coverage becomes available at a reasonable rate, the statute expects Citizens to direct you toward it.

If you are non-renewed by Citizens and cannot find private coverage through the standard admitted market, your next option is the surplus lines market — carriers that operate in Florida but are not admitted by the state. Surplus lines policies are not covered by the Florida Insurance Guaranty Association (FIGA) if the carrier becomes insolvent, which is a meaningful distinction. According to the Florida Insurance Guaranty Association, FIGA provides protection of up to $500,000 per covered claim for policyholders of insolvent admitted carriers. That protection does not extend to surplus lines policyholders.

## Steps to Take Right Now

If you have received a Citizens takeout notice, here is the practical sequence:

1. **Note your opt-out deadline.** Calculate exactly 30 days from the date on the notice, not the date you received it in the mail.

2. **Look up the accepting carrier's AM Best rating and OIR complaint ratio.** Both searches take under five minutes.

3. **Pull the proposed policy form.** Compare it to your current Citizens declarations page section by section — especially roof coverage, water damage, and sinkhole provisions.

4. **Contact a licensed Florida agent and ask for at least two private market quotes.** Give them your current Citizens premium and the takeout offer for comparison.

5. **If you opt out, mark your Citizens renewal date.** The Clearinghouse will run again at renewal, and a private insurer may emerge on the second pass.

6. **Document everything in writing.** If you opt out, do so in writing and keep a copy. If you accept, confirm the transfer date and carrier name in writing.

## FAQ: Citizens Depopulation in Florida

**What is Citizens depopulation in Florida?**
Citizens depopulation is a Florida program that moves Citizens Property Insurance Corporation policyholders to private insurers. Florida law requires Citizens to run depopulation rounds when private carriers agree to accept policies at rates reviewed by the OIR. Policyholders receive a notice and a 30-day window to opt out; those who do nothing are transferred automatically.

**Can I refuse a Citizens depopulation offer?**
Yes. You can opt out of any individual takeout offer by notifying Citizens in writing within the opt-out window. Your policy stays with Citizens through the current term. However, if a comparable private market option exists at or below the statutory rate threshold, Citizens may determine you are no longer eligible for renewal at the end of the current term.

**How much more expensive is a Citizens takeout offer compared to my current rate?**
It varies by property and carrier, but under OIR guidelines the takeout offer cannot be unlimited — there is a cap on how much more a private carrier can charge compared to Citizens. Policyholders have reported offers ranging from a few percentage points to roughly 20% above their Citizens premium. Shopping the broader market independently often surfaces rates that are competitive with or below the takeout offer.

**What happens if I do nothing when I get a Citizens depopulation notice?**
If you do not opt out within the 30-day window, your policy transfers automatically to the accepting private carrier. The new carrier issues a policy, typically at the same renewal date as your original Citizens policy. You do not lose coverage during the transition.

**Is my new private insurer as safe as Citizens?**
Citizens is backed by the state of Florida and can levy assessments on all Florida policyholders in the event of a shortfall — which makes it uniquely stable. Private carriers carry insolvency risk; if they fail, FIGA covers up to $500,000 per claim for admitted carriers only. Checking the accepting carrier's AM Best financial strength rating before accepting any transfer is the single most important due diligence step.

## The Bottom Line

A Citizens depopulation notice is not a crisis — it is a decision. The 30-day window is real, the stakes are real, and the comparison work is straightforward if you know what to look for. The private market for Florida homeowners insurance has improved meaningfully since the reform legislation of 2022 and 2023, and some policyholders are finding that private options are genuinely competitive. Others are finding the takeout offers overpriced relative to the risks. Neither outcome is automatic; it depends on your property, your county, and the carriers bidding in your area.

What is always true: the policyholder who shops, reads the policy form, and checks the carrier's financial strength before the deadline is in a better position than the one who ignores the notice until the window closes.
